# World Models: Definitions, Architectures, Evaluation and Open Problems

## TL;DR

- A world model is an internal predictive model of an environment's dynamics: the canonical generative formulation splits an agent into a vision/encoder, a memory/predictive dynamics model and a controller [1], and this decomposition recurs, in latent form, in the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) family [2][3][4] and in planning-centric learned models [5][6].
- Sample efficiency is the most consistently demonstrated benefit: PlaNet reports outperforming model-free A3C (and in some cases D4PG) on DeepMind Control with "on average 200× less environment interaction" [2], DreamerV2 was reported as the first agent to reach human-level performance on the 55-game Atari benchmark by learning inside a separately trained world model [7], and DreamerV3 was reported to outperform specialized methods across over 150 diverse tasks with a single configuration [4].
- Two competing families now coexist: reconstruction/generative world models that decode observations (RSSM variants, diffusion world models such as DIAMOND [8] and GameNGen [9], and large video generators used as "world simulators" [10][11][12]), versus decoder-free / latent-prediction models in the JEPA tradition that predict representations rather than pixels [13][14][15].
- Interactive, action-conditioned neural simulators became real-time in 2024–2026: GameNGen runs DOOM at 20 FPS on a single TPU with a next-frame PSNR of 29.4 [9], Genie 3 navigates generated worlds at 24 FPS at 720p [16], Matrix-Game 2.0 reports 25 FPS [17], and Zing-0.5 reports 24 FPS at an estimated server cost of roughly USD 0.009 per stream-minute [18].
- Evaluation is fragmented and still the weakest link: newer benchmarks argue against single-number video metrics such as FVD and instead score controllability, physics adherence and dynamics [19][20][21], while explicit limitations are shared across paradigms — auto-regressive error accumulation and exponentially growing action search for planning [15], slow-feature failures when learning latents without reconstruction [22], and failures to model basic physics in large video generators [10].

## Background

A **world model** is an internal predictive model of an environment: the 2018 formulation composed an agent of a visual encoder that compresses each frame into a small latent code, a memory component that predicts future codes, and a decision-making controller that acts only on those representations [1]. The same paper stated the central promise of the paradigm — that an agent can be trained "entirely inside of its own hallucinated dream generated by its world model" and the resulting policy transferred back to the real environment [1]. The recollection-based framing was extended to partially observable settings by implementing the memory component as a recurrent network, with a tiny controller (867 parameters in the linear case) that could be optimized by evolution rather than backpropagation [23].

A 2026 survey characterizes world models as "internal simulators that learn the structure and dynamics of an environment ... enabling agents to predict, plan, and reason within learned representations," dates the modern revival to that 2018 generative demonstration, and organizes the field both by capability and by methodological family (state-space/recurrent, transformer-based, diffusion-based, physics-informed, and language-augmented multimodal systems) [24]. Significance is largely argued on two axes: sample efficiency in control, and the ability to generate or interact with counterfactual futures for planning, simulation and data generation.

Non-generative alternatives exist. The JEPA line argues explicitly for a "non-generative architecture for predictive world models" in which the model "does not actually predict y, but predicts the representation of y," so that encoders can eliminate irrelevant detail [25]; this position was instantiated first for images [13] and then for video [14][15]. These alternatives are surveyed in detail in the thematic sections below.

## Learned latent dynamics and model-based control

The dominant control-oriented design is a latent state-space model learned jointly with reward and value predictors, used to train a policy by imagination. PlaNet introduced the Recurrent State-Space Model with both deterministic and stochastic components, which the authors state was "crucial for high planning performance," and chose actions by online planning with the cross-entropy method rather than using a policy or value network at all; it introduced latent overshooting as a multi-step variational objective [2]. Its headline efficiency result — "on average 200× less environment interaction" than A3C/D4PG on DeepMind Control tasks [2] — remains one of the clearest quantitative statements of the paradigm's value.

Dreamer moved from pure planning to latent imagination: it introduced representation, transition and reward models and learned behaviors by propagating analytic gradients of learned state values back through trajectories imagined in the compact state space [3], arguing that latent states have a "small memory footprint that enables imagining thousands of trajectories in parallel" [3]. DreamerV2 replaced Gaussian latents with categorical variables and reported the first agent to achieve human-level performance on the 55-task Atari benchmark by learning inside a separately trained world model, reaching 200M frames with the same budget and surpassing IQN and Rainbow [7]. DreamerV3 scaled the same idea, claiming to outperform specialized methods across over 150 diverse tasks with a single configuration [4]; on Atari it reports a median score of 302% versus DreamerV2's 219%, and it reports collecting the first diamond in Minecraft from scratch after 29M steps, with 24 of 40 seeds collecting at least one diamond [4].

An alternative control philosophy abandons reconstruction entirely. MuZero learns a model of representation, dynamics and prediction functions that "predicts the quantities most directly relevant to planning: the reward, the action-selection policy, and the value function," explicitly stating that there is "no direct constraint or requirement for the hidden state to capture all information necessary to reconstruct the original observation" [5]; planning is Monte-Carlo Tree Search over the learned latent model, and the method was evaluated on 57 Atari games and on Go, chess and shogi [5]. TD-MPC similarly uses a learned task-oriented latent dynamics model with a terminal value function learned by temporal difference learning for short-horizon trajectory optimization, deliberately learning the latent representation "purely from rewards, ignoring nuances unnecessary for the task at hand," and reports solving Humanoid and Dog locomotion with up to 38-dimensional action spaces in as little as 1M environment steps [26]. TD-MPC2 extends this to 104 online RL tasks spanning four domains with "a single set of hyperparameters," reports that scaling the agent improves capabilities where naive scaling of TD-MPC often degrades them, and trains a single 317M-parameter agent to perform 80 tasks [6].

## Reconstruction-free and latent-prediction world models

The JEPA program argues that a world model should predict representations rather than observations. LeCun's position paper proposes "a non-generative architecture for predictive world models," stating that "the main advantage of JEPA is that it performs predictions in representation space, eschewing the need to predict every detail of y," and enumerates anti-collapse criteria (maximize information about inputs, keep targets predictable, minimize the information content of the prediction latent), plus hierarchical JEPA for longer-term prediction in which "more abstract representations ignore details of the inputs that are difficult to predict in the long term" [25]. I-JEPA instantiated this for images, and reports a concrete efficiency profile: computing targets in representation space is "about 7% slower time per iteration," but the method "converges in roughly 5× fewer iterations," giving compute savings; pretraining a ViT-H/14 is reported as "over 10× more efficient than a ViT-H/14 pretrained with MAE," with a linear-probe top-1 of 81.1 for ViT-H/16_448 versus 77.2 for MAE ViT-H/14 [13].

V-JEPA extended feature prediction to video with no pretrained image encoder, text, negative examples or reconstruction [14], and reported that models trained with feature prediction beat pixel-prediction approaches under frozen attentive probing while being "competitive with pixel prediction under full fine-tuning" despite significantly shorter training schedules [14]. V-JEPA 2 scaled this to over 1 million hours of internet video, then post-trained an action-conditioned predictor (V-JEPA 2-AC, 300M parameters) on less than 62 hours of unlabeled robot interaction data from Droid, deploying zero-shot on Franka arms "without any task-specific training or reward" [15]. Its planning is energy-based rather than generative: the video encoder is frozen, and the action-conditioned predictor is used within a model predictive control loop that minimizes a goal-conditioned energy function with the cross-entropy method and re-plans in receding-horizon fashion [15]. The paper also states the paradigm's rationale directly, arguing that JEPA focuses on predictable aspects such as an object's trajectory "while ignoring unpredictable details that generative objectives emphasize" [15].

Decoder-free designs also appear inside the classic latent-imagination family. MuDreamer learns hidden representations by predicting the value function and previously selected actions instead of pixels, motivated by the observation that although the reconstruction loss is essential to Dreamer's performance, "it also necessitates modeling unnecessary information," causing Dreamer to sometimes fail to perceive crucial elements in the presence of visual distractions; it reports stronger robustness to visual distractions (replacing backgrounds with task-irrelevant real-world video) and comparable Atari100k performance with faster training [27].

The reconstruction-versus-latent question is genuinely contested rather than settled. Sobal et al. found that JEPA methods "perform on par or better than reconstruction when distractor noise changes every time step, but fail when the noise is fixed," presenting this as an important limitation attributable to a slow-feature bias [22]. A separate critique essay argues that the recursive application of latent prediction "is itself functionally autoregressive and generative," that a lossy non-invertible encoder can discard dynamically relevant information with no decoder available to detect it ("if the encoder discards information that turns out to matter for the dynamics being predicted, the world model is learning transitions in an ill-formed state space"), and that evidence for practical latent-prediction usability "remains scarce as these models have mainly been demonstrated in toy environments" [28]. An independent, non-peer-reviewed study on a toy single-dot environment reports a quantified tradeoff: a sufficiently weighted reconstruction loss improved planning from 11–19% to 71–79% on stable backgrounds, while on flickering backgrounds planning dropped by about 4 percentage points, concluding that reconstruction is "a pragmatic fix rather than a pure JEPA solution" and that the tradeoff remains open [29]. Recent robotics work instead adds auxiliary grounding objectives to latent prediction: one approach augments latent prediction with inverse dynamics and state alignment and reports success rates of 100% on TwoRoom, 98% on PushT and 87% on OGBench-Cube [30], while another adds train-only objectives grounding latents in proprioceptive state and joint-angle changes, arguing that pure forward prediction "does not explicitly enforce reliable identifiability of robot-centric physical state from individual latents" [31].

## Generative video models as world simulators

A second, much more compute-intensive family treats large generative video models as world models. OpenAI's Sora report frames text-conditional diffusion models trained jointly on videos and images of variable duration and resolution as world simulators, with the largest model capable of generating a minute of high-fidelity video, and lists failures directly relevant to world modeling: the model "does not accurately model the physics of many basic interactions, like glass shattering," and exhibits "incoherencies that develop in long duration samples or spontaneous appearances of objects" [10]. Notably, model and implementation details are not included in that report, and the Minecraft-controlling capability is described as elicited zero-shot by prompting rather than via an action-conditioned interface [10].

The clearest bridging case is Genie, described as an 11B-parameter unsupervised generative model that creates action-controllable virtual worlds from unlabelled videos, can be prompted with text, synthetic images, photographs or sketches, and enables frame-by-frame control despite training without any ground-truth action labels [32]. Its successors push toward interactivity and duration: Genie 2 is described as an autoregressive latent diffusion foundation world model in which a person or agent supplies keyboard and mouse actions at each step and the model simulates the next observation, generating "consistent worlds for up to a minute, with the majority of examples shown lasting 10-20s" [33]; Genie 3 is described as generating dynamic worlds navigable "in real time at 24 frames per second, retaining consistency for a few minutes at a resolution of 720p," while acknowledging that auto-regressive generation causes "inaccuracies [that] tend to accumulate over time" and stating explicit limits — a limited action space, imperfect geographic accuracy, and support for "a few minutes of continuous interaction, rather than extended hours" [16].

A third strand targets engineering domains. Cosmos defines a world foundation model as predicting the future observation from past observations and a current "perturbation" that can be an action, text description or random perturbation, builds a data pipeline extracting about 100M clips of 2–60 seconds from a 20M-hour video collection, and provides both diffusion and autoregressive transformer models as open-weight models; post-training adapts them to camera control for navigable virtual worlds, robotic manipulation and autonomous driving [12]. In autonomous driving, GAIA-1 casts world modeling as unsupervised next-token prediction over discrete video tokens using video, text and action inputs, with scalar actions representing speed and curvature, and pairs an autoregressive world model with a video diffusion decoder [34]; GAIA-2 shifts to a continuous latent space for improved temporal smoothness and reconstruction fidelity, conditions on ego-vehicle dynamics, agent configurations, environmental factors and road semantics, and generates up to five temporally and spatially consistent camera streams at 448×960 across UK, US and German environments [11]. Cosmos-Drive-Dreams applies a world foundation model platform specifically to synthesizing challenging long-tail driving scenarios [35].

Within reinforcement learning, diffusion has been used as the world-model backbone itself. DIAMOND trains an agent inside a diffusion world model that predicts the next observation conditioned on past observations and discrete actions, reports a mean human-normalized score of 1.46 on Atari 100k — described as a new best for agents trained entirely within a world model — and uses the same architecture to build an interactive neural game engine from 87 hours of static Counter-Strike: Global Offensive gameplay [8]. GameNGen goes further toward a full neural engine, training a diffusion model to produce the next frame conditioned on past frames and actions, running at 20 FPS on a single TPU and stable over multi-minute sessions, with a next-frame PSNR of 29.4 and human raters only slightly better than chance at distinguishing short clips of the real game from the simulation even after five minutes of auto-regressive generation; the paper is explicit that domain shift between teacher-forcing and auto-regressive sampling "leads to error accumulation and fast degradation in sample quality," mitigated here by corrupting context frames with varying Gaussian noise during training [9].

The 2025–2026 wave is characterized by streaming, real-time and openly released interactive models. Matrix-Game 2.0 generates long videos on the fly via few-step auto-regressive diffusion, uses an action-injection module for frame-level mouse and keyboard inputs, leverages about 1200 hours of video data from Unreal Engine and GTA5 environments with interaction annotations, and reports generating minute-level videos at 25 FPS [17]. PAN critiques prompt-to-full-video generation as lacking "causal control, interactivity, or long-horizon consistency" and combines an LLM-based autoregressive latent dynamics backbone with a video diffusion decoder, trained on large-scale video-action pairs for open-domain, action-conditioned simulation [36]. Zing-0.5 describes a 5B autoregressive world model for playability with joint keyboard and online text control, combining four-step generation with context-preserving streaming to support 832×480 inference at 24 FPS at an estimated server cost of approximately USD 0.009 per stream-minute, and reports an overall score of 81.0 and a consistency score of 88.5 across 158 WBench Navigation cases [18]. Outside entertainment and driving domains, CyberWorld applies a Dreamer-style latent world model to autonomous cyber defense, reporting that its graph-based variant exceeds a strategy-agnostic control after 3.6k–15.8k environment steps compared with millions of steps required by model-free PPO [37].

## Evaluation and benchmarks

Evaluation practice splits by community. Video-generation-as-world-model work is measured increasingly by multi-dimensional suites rather than single fidelity scores. WorldScore is presented as "the first unified benchmark for world generation," decomposing the problem into next-scene generation tasks with camera-trajectory layout specifications, using a dataset of 3,000 test examples spanning static/dynamic, indoor/outdoor and photorealistic/stylized worlds, and aggregating controllability, quality and dynamics into ten metrics to evaluate 20 models [20]. WorldModelBench explicitly positions itself against single-number metrics such as Frechet Video Distance and CLIPSIM, adds instruction-following and physics-adherence dimensions that detect "subtle violations, such as irregular changes in object size that breach the mass conservation law," organizes physics adherence into five common violation categories over 350 image/text condition pairs in 7 domains and 56 subdomains, crowdsources 67K human labels to measure 14 frontier models, and reports that a fine-tuned 2B-parameter judger achieves 8.6% higher average accuracy than GPT-4o in predicting world-modeling violations [19] (the paper's introduction instead states a 9.9% lower error rate for the same judger). WorldSimBench proposes a dual framework of explicit perceptual evaluation and implicit manipulative evaluation across open-ended embodied environments, autonomous driving and robot manipulation, with an HF-Embodied dataset of 35,701 tuples carrying multi-dimensional scores and fine-grained human feedback, and assesses "video-action consistency" by testing whether generated situation-aware video can be translated into correct control signals [21].

Control-oriented world models are typically evaluated downstream instead, by the agent's returns or human-normalized score on task suites. Observed suites include Atari 2600 (57 games) and the Atari 100k/200M budgets [5][7][4][8][27], the DeepMind Control Suite [2][3][6][26], Meta-World, ManiSkill2 and MyoSuite [26][6], and Minecraft, ProcGen, DMLab, BSuite and Crafter [4]. Robotics work in the JEPA line uses LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus and SimplerEnv alongside real-world manipulation [38], and goal-conditioned planning evaluations such as TwoRoom, PushT, OGBench-Cube and Reacher [30]. The 2026 survey names "fragmented evaluation practices" as one of the field's persistent challenges alongside compounding prediction errors and sim-to-real transfer [24].

## Applications: driving, robotics and decision-making

Autonomous driving has the most developed world-model application stack: GAIA-1 generates realistic driving scenarios with fine-grained control over ego-vehicle behavior from video, text and action inputs [34]; GAIA-2 generates temporally and spatially consistent multi-camera streams across geographically diverse environments and supports generation from scratch, inpainting, scene editing and long-horizon rollouts [11]; and Cosmos explicitly post-trains its world foundation models for autonomous driving alongside camera control and robot manipulation [12], with Cosmos-Drive-Dreams targeting long-tail safety-critical scenario synthesis [35]. This is also where claims are most carefully hedged: GAIA-2's predecessor relied on discrete latent variables while GAIA-2 uses a continuous latent space [11], and the retrieved GAIA-1 text reports emergent 3D-geometry understanding without reporting benchmark numbers [34].

Robotics is where the latent-prediction family has moved fastest in 2025–2026. V-JEPA 2-AC is post-trained on under 62 hours of unlabeled Droid robot video and deployed zero-shot on Franka arms for reaching, grasping and pick-and-place with image goals, planning by minimizing a learned energy function rather than by generating video [15]. Follow-up work argues that forward latent prediction alone is insufficient for control and adds grounding signals: inverse dynamics and state alignment improved latent transitions' action-informativeness [30], train-time physical-state grounding objectives target identifiability of robot-centric state [31], and a component study across simulated and real robot data reports a model that outperforms the DINO-WM and V-JEPA-2-AC baselines on navigation and manipulation [39]. The same theme appears in vision-language-action models, where predicting action-relevant latent transitions rather than pixel variation is argued to reduce appearance bias and information leakage [38].

Decision-making applications beyond robotics are still sparse but instructive. CyberWorld transfers the Dreamer-style latent world model to network defense, reporting far fewer environment steps than model-free PPO and episodes-to-control that remain "approximately constant as network size increases from 15 to 100 hosts" [37]; this is a custom cybersecurity benchmark rather than a standard control suite, so its numbers are not directly comparable with the Atari/DMC literature.

## Trends and open problems

**Convergence on action-conditioned, interactive generation, with a duration ceiling.** The trajectory from GAIA-1's next-token rollouts [34] and Genie's action-controllable worlds from unlabelled video [32] to real-time interactive systems [9][16][17][18][36] is the clearest recent trend. But the ceiling is explicit and numerical: Genie 3 supports "a few minutes of continuous interaction, rather than extended hours" and retains consistency "for a few minutes" [16], while Genie 2's majority examples last 10–20 seconds [33]. Auto-regressive error accumulation is named as the cause in both the interactive-generation literature [16][9] and the latent-planning literature [15].

**Two paradigms, mutually acknowledged failure modes.** Reconstruction-based models are accused of spending capacity on unpredictable detail — Dreamer "sometimes fails to perceive crucial elements ... when visual distractions are present" [27], and V-JEPA argues pixel-level generative objectives emphasize unpredictable details such as the precise position of each leaf on a tree [15]. Latent-prediction models are accused of throwing away information that matters, with no decoder to diagnose the loss [28], and empirically failing when distractor noise is static rather than varying [22]. Critically, the strongest counter-evidence on both sides is limited in scope: the slow-feature failure was demonstrated in a toy setting [22], the quantitative reconciliation study is an independent non-peer-reviewed toy study [29], and the critique paper itself concedes that evidence for latent-prediction usability "remains scarce as these models have mainly been demonstrated in toy environments" while also judging V-JEPA 2's generalization and scaling to long-horizon, high-complexity environments unclear [28]. Recent robotics papers essentially sidestep the debate by augmenting latent prediction with grounding objectives [30][31][38], which suggests the practical resolution may be hybrid rather than a clean win for either side.

**Evaluation is the field's acknowledged weak point.** Single-number video metrics are explicitly rejected by newer benchmarks in favor of controllability, physics adherence and dynamics [19][20][21], and a 2026 survey lists "fragmented evaluation practices" as a persistent challenge [24]. Physics adherence is now measured through concrete violation categories such as mass conservation breaches [19], reflecting the same failure that Sora's report describes qualitatively as not accurately modeling basic interactions like glass shattering [10]. Nearly all reported wins — PlaNet's 200× interaction reduction [2], DreamerV2's human-level 55-game Atari claim [7], DreamerV3's 302% median Atari score [4], DIAMOND's 1.46 human-normalized score [8], TD-MPC2's 104 tasks with one hyperparameter set [6] — come from each paper's own protocol, and there is no evidence in the retrieved material of a cross-paradigm benchmark that scores generative video world models, latent-prediction world models and control-oriented world models on a common footing.

**Cost and disclosure.** Cost is becoming a first-class reporting dimension only recently: Cosmos documents a 100M-clip pipeline from a 20M-hour collection [12], Matrix-Game 2.0 documents about 1200 hours of driving-game video with interaction annotations [17], and Zing-0.5 is unusual in publishing an estimated server rental cost of roughly USD 0.009 per stream-minute [18]. In contrast, the Sora report omits model and implementation details entirely [10], and the Genie 2 blog does not disclose data sourcing details [33]; several retrieved sources are abstract- or blog-level, so parameter counts, data scales and FPS figures are missing for those entries. Compute cost of diffusion world models is likewise reported qualitatively — DIAMOND notes an "increased computational cost" without a figure [8] — which makes engineering comparisons across families difficult. Scaling behavior is also not monotone: TD-MPC2 reports that naive scaling of TD-MPC often decreases agent performance while TD-MPC2 scaling improves it [6], and DreamerV3 claims that increasing model size translates to both higher task performance and lower data requirements [4].
